● Standout portfolios tend to nail these things:

  Personality & Identity

   - A clear visual identity — consistent colors, typography, and tone (you already have this with the folder aesthetic)
   - Voice that sounds like a real person, not a template (your "about" page does this well)
   - A memorable gimmick or metaphor — desktop OS, terminal, magazine, game world

  Show, Don't Tell

   - Live demos or embedded previews of projects, not just descriptions
   - Short case studies — what was the problem, what did you build, what did you learn
   - Before/after or process shots showing your thinking, not just the finished product

  Craft & Polish

   - Smooth, intentional animations (not flashy for the sake of it)
   - Fast load times — nothing kills a first impression like a slow site
   - Responsive design that feels designed for mobile, not just squeezed to fit
   - Attention to micro-details — hover states, transitions, spacing

  Engagement

   - Something interactive — a mini-game, draggable elements, a quiz, an easter egg
   - A human touch — a blog, a "currently" section (reading, listening to, working on), a changelog/log (you have this!)
   - Easy and obvious way to contact or connect

  Credibility Signals

   - Links to GitHub, LinkedIn, live deployed projects
   - Tech stack clearly listed per project
   - Contributions, metrics, or outcomes when possible ("reduced load time by 40%")

  Things That Separate Good From Great

   - A "now" page — what you're currently learning/building/into (your /log could be this)
   - Curated over comprehensive — 5 great projects > 15 mediocre ones
   - Custom 404 page — shows you care about every detail
   - Accessibility — alt text, keyboard nav, contrast (shows professionalism)
   - Loading state or intro animation — a quick, delightful entrance
